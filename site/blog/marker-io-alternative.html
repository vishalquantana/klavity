The Best Marker.io Alternative for Web Agencies in 2026

Klavity Team7 min read
TL;DRMarker.io is excellent at letting clients annotate and report bugs visually, but it only captures bugs clients notice. Klavity is the best Marker.io alternative for agencies that want AI to find bugs automatically before any client visits the site.

Marker.io makes it easy for clients to report bugs — they click a button, draw on the page, and submit a screenshot with metadata attached. It's a genuinely polished experience. But here's the problem: Marker.io only captures bugs that clients notice and bother to report. For web agencies that want to find issues before the client ever opens the browser, Marker.io isn't enough. You need a Marker.io alternative that catches bugs automatically.

Why do agencies look for Marker.io alternatives?

Agencies typically start looking for a Marker.io alternative when they realise that even with a polished feedback tool in place, clients are still the first to find real bugs. The annotation widget is sitting right there on the page, but clients don't use it consistently — they send a WhatsApp message, or a Slack DM, or they just silently lose trust in the agency.

The underlying issue isn't the feedback tool. It's that the agency's QA process depends on clients to do the testing. That's backwards. Common frustrations:

  • Client adoption drops off: Clients use Marker.io heavily in the first week, then stop.
  • Bugs arrive as surprises: A layout breaks after a deployment and nobody catches it until the client visits three days later.
  • No pre-launch value: Marker.io only works when a client is actively reviewing a live page.
  • Feedback isn't detection: Even 100% client adoption still misses bugs that clients don't notice — accessibility issues, mobile breakpoints, subtle layout shifts.

How is Klavity different from Marker.io?

Klavity is not a feedback tool — it's a bug detection tool. The difference is fundamental. Marker.io waits for a human to notice something wrong and choose to report it. Klavity runs an AI scan on your URL and tells you what's broken before any human visits.

A typical Klavity workflow for a web agency looks like this:

  1. Deploy a site update to staging
  2. Klavity automatically scans the staging URL
  3. You receive a bug report — layout issues, broken interactions, accessibility violations — before the client's review call
  4. Fix the issues before the client ever sees them
  5. Ship to production knowing the site is clean

The result: clients stop being your QA team. They experience a polished site from day one.

When Marker.io is still the right choice

Marker.io excels at one specific thing: giving non-technical clients a structured, low-friction way to communicate feedback. If your clients are actively engaged, give useful notes, and actually use the widget — Marker.io's organised kanban view and Jira/Linear integration make that feedback easy to triage.

It's also useful for design review rounds where the feedback is about copy, colour, or layout preference rather than technical bugs. In those cases, screenshot annotation is exactly what you need.

Stick with Marker.io if client communication and feedback organisation is your primary pain point. Switch to Klavity (or use both) if you're trying to stop clients from being the ones who find bugs.

What about other Marker.io alternatives?

The most common Marker.io alternatives — BugHerd, Userback, Atarim, Pastel — all operate on the same model: make it easier for clients or team members to annotate and submit bug reports. They compete on UI and integrations, not on whether they can find bugs without human input. None of them can.

If your goal is to stop finding out about bugs from clients, you need to move to a different category of tool entirely — one that detects bugs proactively, rather than streamlining the reporting of bugs humans already found.
