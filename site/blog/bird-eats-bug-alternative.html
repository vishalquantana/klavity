Bird Eats Bug Alternative: What Changed After BrowserStack

Klavity7 min read
TL;DRBird Eats Bug is no longer independent: BrowserStack acquired it in August 2024 and rebranded it as Bug Capture by BrowserStack. For a like-for-like screen-recording bug reporter, Jam.dev and Klavity Snap are the closest swaps. If your real problem is clients finding bugs first, you need a tool that tests the site itself rather than a better way to film the failure.

The best Bird Eats Bug alternative depends on what you need it to replace — and the first thing to know is that Bird Eats Bug is no longer an independent product. BrowserStack acquired it in August 2024 and folded the technology into Bug Capture by BrowserStack. If you want a like-for-like swap — a recorder that attaches console and network logs to a bug report — Jam.dev and Klavity Snap are the closest matches. If what you actually need is to stop clients from finding bugs first, you want a tool that tests the site itself, not a better way to film the failure.

What is Bird Eats Bug, and is it still called that?

Bird Eats Bug was a Berlin-based bug-reporting tool built around screen recording. You captured a recording through a browser extension or an embedded web SDK widget, and it automatically attached the technical context a developer needs: console logs, network requests, reproduction steps and environment details. Reports pushed out to Jira, GitHub, Azure DevOps, Slack, Trello and Zapier.

On 27 August 2024, BrowserStack announced it had acquired the company — its fifth acquisition since buying Percy in 2020 — and launched Bug Capture, a manual-testing product built on Bird's technology. Bird Eats Bug has since been rebranded as Bug Capture by BrowserStack. Orrick, which advised on the deal, cited the product as having over 30,000 users at the time.

That matters for anyone evaluating it today. Three practical consequences:

  • You are buying into a platform, not a point tool. Bug Capture is positioned as part of BrowserStack's test platform. BrowserStack's own marketing notes that Bug Capture is now included with BrowserStack plans, so the commercial conversation is increasingly about the wider platform rather than a standalone bug reporter.
  • Product direction is no longer Bird's. Roadmap decisions now sit with an acquirer whose centre of gravity is cross-browser device infrastructure.
  • Vendor performance claims are vendor claims. BrowserStack says Bug Capture helps teams debug issues 30% faster. That is the vendor's own figure, not an independent benchmark — treat it the way you would any marketing number.

What does Bird Eats Bug pricing look like now?

At the time of writing, the published plans start with a free tier of 15 uploads per month that resets at the start of each month, with a time-limited trial of the premium features. Paid plans start at $50/month including five team members, with additional members charged per seat. One detail is genuinely good and worth copying: per their FAQ, bug reporters are free — only admins and members occupy paid seats. That is the right incentive, because the number of people able to file a bug is exactly the number you want to be large.

One caution: the pricing page currently serves conflicting numbers for the higher tiers depending on which variant you load, and third-party directories list figures that disagree with the vendor site. Check the vendor's own pricing page before you budget, and do not trust any blog's number — including the absence of one here for the top tiers.

Why do teams look for a Bird Eats Bug alternative?

Most reasons fall into two buckets, depending on who you are.

If you run a web agency, dev shop or freelance practice

Your bug reporters are usually your clients, and clients do not install browser extensions. The web SDK widget option avoids that, but you are still solving the wrong half of the problem: the report only exists because a client already hit the bug. By then the damage — the "did you even test this?" email — is done. Recording the failure beautifully does not change who found it.

The other common trigger is scope. A bug reporter has nothing to say about regressions. Every time you ship a change to a client site, something that worked last month can quietly stop working, and no amount of capture tooling will notice until a human does.

If you are building with Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, v0 or Replit

Your problem is upstream of reporting entirely. AI-generated code lands faster than you can read it, and your "QA team" is a handful of early users doing you a favour. A tool that files excellent tickets after someone notices a bug cannot help with the bugs nobody noticed — the broken edge case in a flow only 3% of users take, the form that silently fails validation, the auth path that works for you because you are already logged in.

What should you actually look for in a Bird Eats Bug alternative?

Four questions sort the field quickly:

  1. Does the reporter have to install anything? If the people most likely to find your bugs are outside your team, every install step is a bug that gets reported over WhatsApp instead. Prefer an embedded widget over an extension.
  2. Are reporter seats free? Bird got this right. If you pay per person able to file a bug, you will quietly ration the thing you want most of.
  3. Does anything test the product without being asked? This is the dividing line between a bug reporter and a QA system. A recorder is reactive by construction. Persona-driven exploration and self-healing E2E tests are what close the window between shipping and finding out.
  4. Where does the data live? If you build for clients in healthcare, finance or the public sector, "recordings of our admin panel sit in a third-party SaaS" is a procurement conversation you may not win. Self-hosting on any tier, not just enterprise, is worth real money.

Which alternative is right for which team?

  • Already paying for BrowserStack? Bug Capture is probably bundled with your plan. Use it for manual-test evidence and spend your evaluation time elsewhere.
  • Internal dev and QA team who want the richest possible recording? Jam.dev is the closest like-for-like swap — see our Jam.dev comparison for where it stops.
  • Agency whose reporters are clients, and whose real fear is the bug you ship on Friday? You need the reporter and the prevention layer. Snap handles the first, Sims and AutoSim the second.
  • Vibe coder with no QA process at all? Start with autonomous exploration, not reporting. You do not have enough users for a bug reporter to be your safety net.

The capture model — a browser extension or embedded web SDK widget that attaches console logs, network requests and environment details to a recording — is the same technology.

What is the closest like-for-like Bird Eats Bug alternative?

Yes — they solve different halves of the problem.
